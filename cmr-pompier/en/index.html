At the end of every call-out, the crew commander (CA) or incident commander (COS) writes the contamination report for their crew. Command checks that everything is up to date, the occupational health and medical service (SSSM) reviews and completes it, and every firefighter can access their own history.

Why?

A real and recognised exposure

Firefighting as an occupation has been classified as carcinogenic to humans (Group 1) by IARC (International Agency for Research on Cancer) since 2022. Whatever is not recorded on the day of the call-out is lost, both for medical follow-up and for any future recognition as an occupational disease.

Ready for your obligations

What the regulations require, VB CMR produces

The reports completed after each call-out feed directly into the documents the texts require. The SSSM doctor checks, signs off, then downloads.

DGSCGC circular (circulaire DGSCGC) · 14 January 2025

Annual record and exposure certificate

An annual record of potentially exposing activities for each firefighter, before the medical check-up, and a cumulative certificate to be kept for 50 years. Separate templates for career and volunteer firefighters.

Generated and signed off by the doctor in the SSSM area
Decree no. 2025-1349 (décret n° 2025-1349) · 26 December 2025

Training, overhaul and cleaning count too

Occupational disease tables no. 16 bis and 30 (tableaux n° 16 bis et 30) cover firefighting, including exposing training, overhaul and equipment cleaning.

Smoke-house sessions, live-fire training and equipment cleaning, declared in one form
French Labour Code (Code du travail) · R. 4412-93-1

List of exposed workers and individual record

Nature, duration and degree of each exposure to CMR agents, sent to the health service and kept for at least 40 years.

Individual record by year, over 12 months or over the whole career
