1Employer obligations

Assessing and tracing CMR exposure

Knowing which CMR agents are present, who is exposed, under which conditions, and keeping that information for decades: everything else builds on it.

Inventory

List every carcinogenic, mutagenic or reprotoxic substance, mixture, process and by-product present or emitted, and assess the type, level and duration of exposure.

H340H350H360Processes
French Labour Code, art. R. 4412-61 · Directive 2004/37/EC, art. 3
With VB SafetyInventory per site and workstation, hazard statements, processes and by-products (fumes, dust).

Register of exposed workers

Keep an up-to-date list of workers liable to be exposed, with the agents concerned and, where known, the type, duration and level of their exposure.

Art. R. 4412-93-1 ff. · decree 2024-307 · Directive 2004/37/EC, art. 12
With VB SafetyRegister generated from workstations and tasks, updated at every change.

40-year retention

Exposure records are kept for at least forty years.

Decree 2024-307 · Directive 2004/37/EC, art. 15
With VB SafetyTime-stamped, archived history, exportable at any time.

Transmission

Send the register to the occupational health service, inform each worker of the data that concerns them and share an anonymised version with the works council.

Art. R. 4412-93-1 ff.
With VB SafetyExports ready for the occupational health service, individual sheet per worker, anonymised version for the works council.
2Employer obligations

The prevention hierarchy, in order

For CMR agents, measures are not a matter of choice: the law sets an order. You move to the next step only if the previous one is technically impossible.

From most effective to last resort

1
SubstitutionRemove the hazard

Replace the CMR agent with a non-hazardous or less hazardous substance, mixture or process whenever technically possible. The employer records the results of this search.

Art. R. 4412-66 · Directive 2004/37/EC, art. 4
Documented substitution study for each agent, with the justification kept when it is impossible.
2
Closed systemEnclose the process

If substitution is impossible, the CMR agent is produced and used in a closed system.

Art. R. 4412-68 · Directive 2004/37/EC, art. 5
Closed-system workstations and equipment identified in the inventory.
3
ReductionLimit exposure

Reduce the quantities used, the number of exposed workers and the intensity of exposure to the lowest technically possible level: local exhaust ventilation, demarcated areas, signage, hygiene measures.

Art. R. 4412-69 and R. 4412-70
Action plan per workstation, with owners and deadlines.
4
ProtectionCollective first, then individual

Collective protection and hygiene come first. Personal protective equipment is the last resort, when exposure cannot be avoided otherwise.

Art. R. 4412-70
Collective protection and PPE linked to each exposed workstation.
3Employer obligations

Checks, health surveillance and restrictions

Measure regularly, follow the health of exposed workers and make sure nobody is assigned to a post they are barred from.

Occupational exposure limit checks

In France, compliance with occupational exposure limits (OELs) is checked at least once a year by an accredited body. If a binding limit is exceeded, work stops at the posts concerned until corrective measures are in place.

Art. R. 4412-76 ff.
With VB SafetyCheck calendar, archived reports from the accredited body, alerts before due dates.

Enhanced health surveillance

In France, workers exposed to CMR agents receive enhanced individual surveillance: a fitness examination before assignment, renewed by the occupational physician at least every four years, with an interim visit after two years at the latest.

Art. R. 4624-23 and R. 4624-28
With VB SafetyVisit due dates per worker, with no medical data stored.

Assignment restrictions

  • Workers under 18 : work exposing them to hazardous chemical agents is prohibited, except supervised derogations for training (art. D. 4153-17).
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding workers : not assigned to posts exposing them to category 1A or 1B reprotoxic agents, benzene and certain aromatic derivatives (art. D. 4152-10).
  • Fixed-term and temporary workers : certain work involving very hazardous agents is prohibited for them (art. D. 4154-1).
With VB SafetyAssignment restrictions shown each time a worker is linked to an exposed post.
Regulation

Key texts

CMR risk falls under specific rules, stricter than those for other hazardous chemical agents, and regularly strengthened.

  1. Directive 2004/37/ECEuropean Union

    The EU framework for carcinogens and mutagens

    Amended several times, it has also covered reprotoxic substances since Directive (EU) 2022/431 and sets binding occupational exposure limit values.

  2. Art. R. 4412-59 to R. 4412-93French Labour Code

    Specific rules for preventing CMR risks

    Assessment, substitution, closed systems, exposure reduction, OEL checks, information, training and traceability.

  3. 1 January 2021Order of 26 October 2020

    The list of carcinogenic processes grows

    The French list of carcinogenic substances, mixtures and processes now includes work exposing to respirable crystalline silica dust generated by a work process.

  4. 5 July 2024Decree 2024-307

    The register of workers exposed to CMR agents

    Up-to-date register of exposed workers with the type, duration and level of exposure, sent to the occupational health service and kept for at least forty years. The decree also tightened limit values for benzene, acrylonitrile and nickel compounds.

10 000 €

This is the fine for a breach of health and safety rules in France, applied once per worker concerned (art. L. 4741-1 of the Labour Code). The employer may also be held liable for inexcusable fault in case of occupational disease.

Frequently asked questions

CMR agents: what employers ask us

What is a CMR agent?

A carcinogenic, mutagenic or reprotoxic agent. EU and French rules cover substances and mixtures classified in categories 1A or 1B (hazard statements H350, H340 or H360), plus substances, mixtures and processes listed by regulation, such as work exposing to hardwood dust or respirable crystalline silica.

What are the employer's obligations for CMR risk?

Assess exposure, substitute the agent whenever technically possible, otherwise work in a closed system, reduce exposure to the lowest possible level, give priority to collective protection, check occupational exposure limits, inform and train workers, ensure health surveillance and keep the register of exposed workers.

What must the register of exposed workers contain?

The identity of each worker liable to be exposed, the CMR agents concerned and, where known, the type, duration and level of exposure. In France this applies since 5 July 2024; the EU directive requires a list of exposed workers (art. 12).

How long must exposure data be kept?

At least forty years after exposure ends, under Directive 2004/37/EC (art. 15) and French rules.

Who receives the register of exposed workers?

In France, the occupational health service, which adds it to the worker's occupational health record. Each worker is informed of their own data, and the works council receives an anonymised version.

How often must occupational exposure limits be checked?

In France, at least once a year by an accredited body, and after any change in installations or working conditions likely to affect exposure.

Who may be assigned to a post exposed to CMR agents?

In France, work exposing to hazardous chemical agents is prohibited for workers under 18, except supervised derogations for training. Pregnant or breastfeeding workers may not be assigned to posts exposing to category 1A or 1B reprotoxic agents. Some very hazardous work is prohibited for fixed-term and temporary workers.

Does the portal store medical data?

No. It manages agents, workstations, exposures and due dates. Medical results stay with the occupational physician.

When will the portal be available?

It is being designed with HSE teams. Ask for early access: we present the solution and give you access among the first users.
